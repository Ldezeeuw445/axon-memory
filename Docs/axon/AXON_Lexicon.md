# The AXON Lexicon

Built on fixed canon — the Hero Film, the Physical & Motion Design Language, the Experience Principles, the Product Language, the Memory Thread, the Core. This document names what already exists. It invents nothing that isn't already required by the mechanics already established, and it deliberately stops before it invents anything more.

The governing instruction throughout: reduce, don't expand. If an existing AXON word can explain something, no new word gets created to explain it a second time.

---

## 1. Core Vocabulary — the permanent concepts

Six words. Not seven, not five — six is where the mechanics already established actually stop needing new names.

### Memory
**Definition.** The smallest unit — one thing that mattered enough to be kept.
**Why it exists.** Every larger concept in this system is built out of these; nothing above this level can be defined without it.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** There isn't a competing word to reject here — "memory" is deliberately the one plain, honest, unownable word in the set. Distinctiveness comes from what AXON does with it, not from disguising it in a coined term.
**When people encounter it.** Constantly, and almost always implicitly — rarely said aloud as a label, mostly felt as the thing that was true without having to be restated.

### Thread
**Definition.** A continuous strand of connected memories about one thing — a person, a project, a decision, an idea.
**Why it exists.** It's the one object that replaces files, folders, and chats simultaneously — without it, the whole system collapses back into needing containers.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** "Conversation" implies a start and end. "File" implies a format. "Topic" is inert and doesn't imply accumulation over time. Thread is the only word that's already about continuity without any editing.
**When people encounter it.** Whenever they're looking at, adding to, or returning to anything specific — the single most common noun in the entire vocabulary.

### Core
**Definition.** The living entity that holds every Thread and grows through everything a person keeps.
**Why it exists.** Something has to be the "it" that's growing — without a Core, growth has nowhere to be witnessed.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** "Assistant," "model," and "AI" all describe a tool being operated. Core describes something a relationship happens *with* — closer to a place than a product.
**When people encounter it.** Rarely by name in daily use — mostly at the handful of moments the whole system reveals itself at once (onboarding, a milestone, a genuine transition).

### Facet
**Definition.** A specific view or ability of the Core — what "AXON Companion" or "Trading OS" actually are, structurally.
**Why it exists.** Without it, each product needs its own name for "the part of AXON I'm currently using," and the whole point — one intelligence, many abilities — gets lost the moment two different words are needed for it.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** "App," "module," and "tool" all imply separate, swappable software. Facet implies the same object seen from a different angle, which is the literal truth of how the system works.
**When people encounter it.** Whenever moving between products — never announced, just felt as a different facet catching the light differently.

### Weight
**Definition.** How much something matters, expressed as mass rather than a rating.
**Why it exists.** Significance needs a physical vocabulary or it collapses into stars, checkmarks, and priority flags — exactly the dashboard language this whole system is built to avoid.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** "Priority," "importance," and "relevance" are all administrative words that belong to task-management software. Weight is the only one that's physical rather than clerical.
**When people encounter it.** Never as a number. Felt entirely through how close, how slow, and how settled something behaves.

### Depth
**Definition.** How time behaves inside AXON — history as something excavated, never scrolled.
**Why it exists.** Without it, the system needs a timeline, and a timeline is recency-biased by definition — the opposite of how memory that actually matters behaves in a real life.
**Why no existing word is good enough.** "History," "timeline," and "log" are all flat, chronological, and administrative. Depth is the only spatial word available, and time in this system is spatial.
**When people encounter it.** Whenever going back further than the immediate present — always felt as going deeper, never as scrolling further.

## 2. The Product Dictionary — what survives, what doesn't

Working through the full candidate list, including the words already accounted for above:

- **Thread, Core, Memory, Weight, Depth — survive**, as defined above.
- **Facet — survives**, added to the set. It wasn't on the original candidate list, but it's the merge target below and it already exists in the physical canon (the Core's shape is literally faceted).
- **Lens — disappears, merged into Facet.** Both describe "a way of viewing the same underlying thing." Two words for one idea is exactly the redundancy this exercise exists to remove, and Facet already has a physical, canon-backed home; Lens would be a second, competing metaphor system (optics, next to mineralogy) for no added meaning.
- **Recall — survives, but as a verb, not a noun.** There's no standalone "a Recall" as a thing; there's only the act of recalling. Demoted to Section 3.
- **Connection — disappears as a noun.** The verb "Connect" survives. The underlying mechanic (two things drawn into a shared orbit) stays internal design language — a person never needs the word "orbit" to feel two things are related.
- **Context — disappears entirely as a proprietary term.** This is the one deliberate rejection worth explaining directly: "context" is already the single most overused word in the entire AI industry — context window, context length, "adding context" — used by every competitor, constantly. Keeping it as an AXON concept would mean sharing the industry's most generic word as one of only six permanent ones. The plain English word remains usable in ordinary sentences, the way any word is; it is never capitalized, never a UI label, never treated as something AXON invented or owns.
- **Continuity — survives, but reclassified.** It doesn't name a discrete thing or action inside the product — it names what the whole product is *for*. It belongs to marketing and the tagline register (Section 5), not the six interaction words above.

## 3. Verbs — only the ones that deserve to exist

Nine, each doing a job none of the others do. One candidate was cut outright; two apparent overlaps were resolved by disambiguation rather than by picking a winner and discarding the other.

- **Capture** — the moment a memory is kept. Never gated by a form, a category, or a folder choice.
- **Recall** — the system bringing something back into view on its own, at a transition, because it's genuinely relevant now.
- **Connect** — two Threads drawn into a shared orbit, whether inferred quietly or endorsed by a deliberate gesture.
- **Weave** — many small memories fusing into one denser point inside an existing Thread. Fine-grained, usually automatic. Replaces the placeholder word "transform" used in earlier canon.
- **Anchor** — a person deliberately marking something as permanently significant, overriding the system's own computed Weight. The deliberate counterpart to Weight the same way a deliberate Connect gesture is the counterpart to an inferred one.
- **Settle** — the ongoing, gradual sinking of something inactive toward the permanent base. Not an event — a state that continues indefinitely once it begins.
- **Retire** — the moment a Thread's active life ends. An event, not an ongoing state — after retiring, a Thread settles, forever.
- **Restore** — a person deliberately reviving something that had already retired and settled. Distinct from Recall: Recall never requires something to have left active use in the first place.
- **Merge** — two entire Threads discovered to be the same underlying thing, combined into one. The coarse-grained, usually deliberate counterpart to Weave's fine-grained, usually automatic fusion of individual memories.

**Cut:** **Surface.** Near-synonymous with Recall, and closer to generic enterprise-software language ("surfacing insights") than to anything specific to AXON. One verb for "bring something back into view" is enough.

## 4. Forbidden Language

Two kinds of ban: the words never used, and the behaviors that would smuggle a banned word back in under a permitted one.

**Never used, anywhere, in any register:** Chat. Conversation. Folder. Workspace. Knowledge Base. Assistant. Bot. Copilot. Database. History. Archive. Prompt.

**Added to that list, made necessary by canon already fixed:** Notification. Dashboard. Session. Sync. Import. Feed. Tag. Category. Alert.

**The deeper rule.** A Thread should never become a chat under a different name — and the risk isn't that someone types the word "chat" into the interface. It's that a Thread's *behavior* quietly turns chat-shaped while keeping its permitted name: a scrollable list of timestamped messages, a "jump to latest" affordance, a sense of a session with a beginning. The test that catches this isn't lexical, it's behavioral: **if a Thread ever needs a "scroll to the bottom to see what's new" motion, it has already become a chat, regardless of what it's called.** The word is the easy part to police. The behavior is the part that actually matters.

## 5. Public vs. Internal Language

**Public** — what a person actually encounters: Memory, Thread, Core, Facet, Capture, Recall, Connect, Weave, Anchor, Settle, Retire, Restore, Merge.

**Marketing** — a slightly wider register, used in copy and the film but never inside the product itself: Continuity, and phrases built from it ("the future of human continuity," "one memory, everywhere"). Marketing has more poetic license than the interface does, but it may never contradict or dilute the six core words — it can only ever be a more elevated way of saying the same thing.

**Developer** — the API and SDK layer, for anyone building against AXON. The same six nouns and nine verbs, in the same logic, just in code-appropriate form (a `thread` object, a `weight` score, a `facet` endpoint). No parallel vocabulary is ever invented for developers — the discipline holds end to end, or it doesn't hold at all.

**System** — internal working language for the team only, never exposed externally in any form: *Orbit* for the connection mechanic, *Void* for the base environment, *Chord* for the sound asset, *the base* or *sediment* for the settled-memory layer. These are working metaphors that keep the team's thinking consistent; a person using the product never needs to know any of them.

**Engineering** — literal code and schema naming. This deliberately isn't a separate vocabulary from Developer — the database calls it `thread`, the class is called `Thread`, and no internal renaming is permitted between what the API says and what the code says. The most common way a product's language quietly rots is a gap between what marketing calls something and what the schema calls it; here, there is no gap by design.

## 6. Tone of Voice

**Sentence rhythm.** Short and declarative. Rarely more than one clause. Confidence comes from brevity, never from qualifiers.

**Confidence.** States things as true, never as pitched. No "we believe," no "designed to help," no hedge language of any kind — the standard SaaS reflex of softening every claim into a marketing promise.

**Emotional tone.** Warm because familiar, not warm because performing enthusiasm. Closer to how you'd speak to someone you respect and have known a long time than to how a brand addresses a customer.

**Punctuation.** Periods. An occasional em dash for a single breath. Almost never an exclamation point. Almost never a question — asking is reserved for people asking AXON something, mirroring the product rule that AXON itself asks questions only rarely.

**Terminology.** Only the words in this lexicon. If a sentence needs an outside word to explain something, that's a sign either the lexicon is missing a real concept, or the sentence is trying to explain what should have been felt instead.

**What AXON never says.** No "Oops!" No "Just a sec…" No apology performed as personality. No first-person-plural announcement voice ("We're excited to..."). And, deliberately, AXON almost never calls itself "an AI" in its own voice — it can be described that way by others, but its own language doesn't use the word about itself, because the entire positioning is that it isn't a member of that category.

## 7. Future-Proofing — surviving the disappearance of screens

The test applied to every word: does it depend on a screen, a cursor, or a gesture specific to this decade's hardware to make sense? All six nouns and all nine verbs pass, and it's not an accident — every one of them was drawn from physical, spatial language (a strand, a weight, a depth, a settling, an anchor) rather than interface language (a click, a swipe, a scroll, a tap). Physical metaphors don't expire when the hardware does. Interface metaphors always do — which is exactly why gestures and screen-specific interactions, wherever they were named in earlier canon, are treated as implementation details of this decade, never as part of the permanent lexicon. When voice, spatial, and ambient computing replace the screen, every word here should still be sayable, out loud, and mean exactly the same thing.

## 8. Vocabulary Stress Test

Applied honestly — including the places a word doesn't pass cleanly.

- **Memory.** Could Apple have invented it? Yes, and so has everyone else — it's already the most common word in the category. That's the point: it survives *because* it's plain and unownable, not despite it. Distinctiveness comes from the other five words, which reframe what it means structurally.
- **Thread.** The real risk here is collision, not genericness — "thread" already means a sequential list of messages in email and forums, which is precisely the meaning being rejected. The word survives only if the behavior never lets it collapse back into that meaning (see Section 4's behavioral test). This is a live risk to keep watching, not a solved problem.
- **Core.** Clean pass on every criterion. Plausibly Apple, not generic in this usage, not meaningfully copyable without copying the underlying mechanic, a child understands "the middle of something," ten-year durable.
- **Facet.** The one honest weak spot: a young child may not know the word itself, even though the underlying idea — one flat face of a cut stone — is instantly graspable once shown. It survives the test anyway because there's no simpler word that keeps the physical precision without losing the meaning; "side" or "part" would both quietly reopen the door to "app" or "feature."
- **Weight, Depth.** Clean passes — plain, ancient, physical words that were never available for anyone to own in the first place, which is exactly why nobody can take them away.

## The AXON Lexicon — final reference

**The six words.** Memory · Thread · Core · Facet · Weight · Depth

**The nine verbs.** Capture · Recall · Connect · Weave · Anchor · Settle · Retire · Restore · Merge

**Never used.** Chat · Conversation · Folder · Workspace · Knowledge Base · Assistant · Bot · Copilot · Database · History · Archive · Prompt · Notification · Dashboard · Session · Sync · Import · Feed · Tag · Category · Alert

**The one behavioral test that matters more than any word ban:** if it ever needs scrolling to the bottom to see what's new, it has already become the thing this lexicon exists to prevent.

**The governing rule for everything not yet written:** if an existing word already explains it, no new word gets invented to explain it twice.

---

*This lexicon governs the language of the website, the applications, the film, the documentation, the marketing, and every interface built after it. Every future word proposed for AXON is measured against what's already here before it's allowed to exist.*
