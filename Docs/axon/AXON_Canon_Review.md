# The AXON Canon Review

Final internal review before this leaves the building. Not a creator's pass — a protector's. Everything below was found by re-reading all five documents against each other, line by line, not by re-imagining any of them.

---

## The Ten Lenses

**1. Philosophy.** The thesis holds — it doesn't need to get smaller, it needs to stop contradicting itself in three specific places, all found by cross-referencing documents rather than reading any one in isolation:
- Product Principle #12 still says "one store, many **lenses**" — the Lexicon killed "Lens" in favor of "Facet" after that principle was written. One word, two documents, two answers.
- The Hero Film's own emotional arc ends at *"I want this."* The Experience document explicitly argues, with real reasoning, that the arc should end one step further at *"I don't want to lose this."* Two canonical documents assert two different endings for the same journey.
- Product Language Section 3 is titled **"Context Instead Of Conversations"** — built entirely around elevating "Context" to a proprietary concept. The Lexicon later explicitly rules that Context can never be an AXON-owned term. The section's own name now argues against a decision made after it.

**2. Story.** The arc is right. Two real findings: the film currently has three different beats each independently trying to be "the biggest moment" (the Core's formation, the ecosystem wide shot, the recontextualized ending) — a 70-second film can't hold three peaks without flattening all of them. And the fix for the original starfield cliché — one hard light through haze — is a genuine improvement, but it's worth naming honestly that volumetric light-through-haze is itself a fairly common premium-tech visual; it survives only because of what it's revealing, not because the technique itself is rare.

**3. Physical Language.** Two rules exist because they're clever, not because they're necessary. The "one worn facet per user" mechanic is beautiful and completely unspecified — there's no answer anywhere for what usage signal determines *which* facet wears, which means it can't actually be built as written. And Section 6 gives Trading OS's chip a "waveform-like cut" — the exact motif Section 2 explicitly bans as assistant-avatar language a few pages earlier in the same document.

**4. Experience.** Two of the fifteen principles are restatements of others wearing different clothes, and one doesn't belong on this list at all. Detailed below.

**5. Product.** Thread, Core, Facet survive clean. Weight and Depth genuinely overlap — both are spatial metaphors for significance — but they answer different grammatical questions (a property vs. a navigational axis) and the distinction is real enough to keep both, not merged out of nervousness.

**6. Vocabulary.** The Lexicon's own stress test already did most of this work honestly. The one thing it missed: its own ruling on "Context" directly contradicts a section title written earlier in canon (see Philosophy, above).

**7. Emotional.** The most clever thing in this entire project might be the project itself. Five documents, a formal lexicon, a stress test, thirty-five numbered principles across two lists — for a product that hasn't shipped. That volume of internal philosophy is itself worth questioning under the brief's own rule: *if something exists only because it's clever, remove it.*

**8. Longevity.** The single biggest risk in the whole canon: the cold open's entire emotional wound — the fatigue of re-explaining yourself to an amnesiac AI — is a symptom of a *closing industry gap*, not a permanent human condition. If memory becomes a commodity feature across the industry in the next few years, that specific wound stops being universal. The parts that don't depend on today's AI landscape at all — the materials, the sound, the mark, "memory changes texture never content" — are the parts confirmed to survive to 2036. The parts that argue against "chat" specifically are reasoning against a comparison point that may itself not be the dominant paradigm in ten years, even though the words chosen (Thread, Weight, Depth) survive that shift fine.

**9. Dieter Rams.** The system passes the real Rams test — a small number of consistent physical laws generating a lot of behavior is exactly how "less but better" actually works; a Braun product's minimalism was never an absence of internal complexity, it was disciplined rules generating it. The one place this fails is the worn-facet mechanic: it's the single arbitrary, unspecified exception introduced into a system that otherwise earns everything through existing laws. Sound design has slightly more surface area than necessary — five distinct sound behaviors for a v1 identity is over-designed by Rams' own standard.

**10. Brutal.** Forgetting who made this: another studio handed a five-document, two-list, one-lexicon canon for a product that doesn't exist yet would get one question first, before any note on the work itself — *how much of this time went into describing the feeling instead of building the thing that produces it?* That question doesn't have a design answer. It's the finding that matters most.

## KEEP

**The six-beat film skeleton** — cold open, void, Core awakens, proof/personal, proof/builder, ecosystem, mark. Chaos to order to proof to scale to silence. Untouched.

**The Core as a living mineral object, never a face.** The single most valuable asset in the project. No note.

**The Thread as the one primitive.** No files, no folders, no chats, no separate containers — everything else in the product language is a consequence of this one substitution, not a list of unrelated features.

**Weight and Depth, held as distinct.** One is a property, one is a navigational axis. The overlap is real and the distinction is real too.

**Facet**, despite being the weakest word on the child-comprehension test. There is no simpler replacement that doesn't quietly reopen the door to "app" or "feature."

**The unbroken-line mark, derived rather than designed** — traced from an actual frame of Core-formation footage. The method of finding it, not just the shape, is what makes it ownable.

**The sonic arc's governing rule** — one loud moment, earned by everything before it, never repeated at full volume. This is the single load-bearing rule in the entire sound identity; everything else in Section 8 is downstream of it.

**Retirement instead of deletion. Memory changes texture, never content. One store, many facets** (wording corrected below). **Discovered, never loaded.** These four are the ones that showed up correctly, independently, in multiple documents without ever contradicting each other — the actual proof the philosophy is coherent.

**The six Lexicon nouns and nine verbs**, and the forbidden list, unchanged. The Lexicon did its own reduction work already and it holds.

---

## REMOVE

**The waveform motif on the Trading OS chip.** It directly reuses language explicitly banned two sections earlier in the same document for the same reason it was banned the first time. Replace with a candle-chart-derived facet pattern; candles were never banned, waveforms were.

**Two of the fifteen Experience Principles, as standalone entries.** "No status-light language" (#7) is a material/color rule, not a felt-experience rule — it already lives correctly in the Physical Language Constitution and doesn't need a second, separately-worded home. "Nothing arrives from the edge of the screen" (#9) is an implementation detail of "discovered, never loaded" (#1), not an independent law. Neither idea disappears — they're removed only as *duplicate entries*, folded back into the one place each already belongs.

**Every second and third restatement of the same four or five rules across documents.** "No mascot" is independently asserted in three separate places. "No status-light color" is stated near-verbatim three times. "No confetti or celebration" is stated four times, in four slightly different sentences. None of these ideas are wrong — the repetition is the problem. As more people write more documents against this canon over time, three slightly different phrasings of one rule is exactly how internal inconsistency starts. Each rule gets exactly one canonical sentence, living in the Constitution, referenced everywhere else — never re-derived in a new document's own words again.

**The section-7 "trust" elaboration in the Creative Direction document.** The Experience document's Trust section already covers this ground in far more depth. Cut it down to the two-line cross-reference the Physical Language document already models correctly for its own Website Architecture section — point at the canonical version, don't repeat it.

---

## SIMPLIFY

**Product Principle #12** — "one store, many lenses" becomes "one store, many facets." A wording fix, not a concept change, correcting the one place canon didn't catch up with its own later decision.

**Product Language Section 3's title** — "Context Instead Of Conversations" becomes "Position Instead Of Conversations." The idea underneath (a field of threads positioned by relevance, replacing a chronological chat list) never actually needed the word "context" to make its point — "position" was always the load-bearing word in that section, and now the title matches the vocabulary the Lexicon actually settled on.

**The film's ending emotion.** Standardize on "I don't want to lose this" everywhere — it's the more rigorously argued of the two endings, and the Creative Direction document's beat-by-beat journey should be brought in line with it rather than left as a second, weaker answer sitting uncontested in an earlier document.

**The three competing "biggest moment" claims**, made explicit rather than left to quietly compete. The Core's formation and its chord are the one true peak. The ecosystem wide shot and the recontextualized ending are stated, plainly, as secondary beats that borrow their power from that peak rather than each trying to independently be the biggest moment in the film.

**Sound Identity, from five distinct behaviors to four.** "Interaction sound" (opening a chip) and "confirmation sound" (an action completing) merge into one acknowledgment tone. The distinction between "you opened something" and "you confirmed something" is unlikely to be perceptible to anyone, expensive to keep consistent forever, and not worth two separate sounds.

**The worn-facet mechanic, given one real trigger.** Instead of an unspecified "derived from a person's actual use," tie it to the single metric the system already tracks for everything else — total accumulated Weight, or account age. The idea survives; the part that made it unbuildable disappears.

**The cold open's dependency on a single wound.** Keep the re-explaining-yourself moment — it's the strongest opening the film has — but let the void's reveal lean at least as hard on *fragmentation across tools* as on *forgetting*. Individual AI memory is a feature every competitor can eventually ship. One memory across every tool a person uses is not. The film's opening wound should point at the harder problem, not just the currently-unsolved one.

---

If AXON launched tomorrow, the single greatest remaining weakness that could still prevent it from becoming an iconic product is this: the canon is more finished than the proof that anyone wants to live inside it — and the wound the entire hero film is built on has a shelf life the canon has never priced in.
